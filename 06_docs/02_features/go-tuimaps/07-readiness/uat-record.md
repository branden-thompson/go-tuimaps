**A flaw in the order, found during the sitting and recorded rather than smoothed over.**
The key is per *scenario*, but the sitting has four screens per scenario. Once the key is
shown after the first screen, the remaining three are no longer read blind, and the rule
this record is built on - "an answer written after the key is not evidence" - no longer
holds for them. From scenario 2 onward the order is: **read all four frames and write all
four readings, then show the key once, then give four verdicts.** Scenario 1's second
reading is marked above as not blind, and its third and fourth will be too.

## Finding 1 - the description is correct but not how a person would say it (14.15, scenario 2)

**Raised by HUM LEAD during the sitting, against the description and not against any frame.**
Verbatim: *"Descriptions work for tests, but in terems of human understandable, they would
not be what a human would expect. Looking at this picture, I would expext this
'description' to be something like, 'A zone alert encompasses the entirety of <county
name> county. The city of <portland? seattle?> is also the zone-alert.'"*

What the description says today is **geometric**: which side of the area the place is on,
how far the nearest edge is, and in which compass direction. What HUM LEAD expected is
**toponymic**: the named places and administrative areas the alert covers, and which named
places fall inside it.

Both are true statements about the same data. The geometric form is what M1b is checked
against, by an independent key that shares no code with the library (D-43), and that
checking is what makes the numbers trustworthy. But a person reading a weather alert
reasons in names, not in bearings - and this library exists to be read by people through a
host.

**Not resolved here.** It is a design question for REVIEW, and it carries a scope decision
for v0.1.0 that is HUM LEAD's: whether the tag ships the geometric description with the
toponymic one as later work, or whether the description is widened first. A first read of
feasibility, to be checked and not relied on: the kept layers already carry `place` names
and a `boundary` layer with administrative levels, so naming *cities inside an area* looks
within reach from data the library already decodes; naming *the county an alert covers*
needs named administrative polygons, which the kept boundary layer may not carry. That
difference decides how large the work is, and it has not been established yet.

## Finding 2 - in no colour the radar bands cover the borders (14.15 and 14.18, scenario 3)

**Raised by HUM LEAD, blind, on the 149x38 no-colour frame.** Verbatim: *"Same image
OUTSIDE but black and grey - the grey bands cover the borders completely."*

In colour the radar bands are read as tints over a basemap that still shows through. In no
colour they are greys, and HUM LEAD read them as hiding the borders underneath rather than
sitting over them. The place could still be judged outside the rain, so the scenario's own
question survived; what was lost is the basemap context a person uses to say *where* the
rain is. That context is what Finding 1 is also about, from the other side.

A second observation from the same scenario, and **the coordinator's first reading of it
was wrong**: it was recorded as a difference between *sizes*, three bands at 149x38 read as
two at 69x12. HUM LEAD settled it, and it is a difference of **colour depth, not size**:
*"Colored pictures had 3 bands, greyscale images had 2 with the most intense being
distinguishable."* **The no-colour radar ramp carries two levels where colour carries
three.** The heaviest band stays distinguishable, so the scenario's question is still
answerable, but one level of intensity is lost when colour is taken away. That is a real
NFR-15 finding and not a property of the smaller frame.

Both belong to NFR-15's no-colour reading (plan task 14.18) and are for REVIEW.

## Finding 3 - distance is in the words, not in the picture (14.15, scenario 3)

**HUM LEAD, after the key:** *"Description does give distance, but again human watchers
would have a hard time distinguishing the exact distance."*

The same shape as Finding 1 and as scenario 2's result: the picture places a thing, the

## Finding 4 - a field's no-colour form carries its shape but not its values (14.15 and 14.18, scenario 4)

**Raised by HUM LEAD, blind, on both no-colour frames.** Verbatim: at 149x38, *"I see the
outlines, but there's no grey bands"*; at 69x12, *"no grey scales, just lines of the temp
zones"*. HUM LEAD's readings are numbered 3a to 3d in the sitting but are scenario 4.

**The coordinator first wrote this finding up as a possibly missing form and was wrong.**
It was checked in the code before the finding was allowed to stand. `internal/render/field.go`
draws a field with no ramp as **contour lines**, and a raster with no ramp as the block
shades U+2591 to U+2593, both under D-59. So the radar's greys in scenario 3 and the
temperature's bare lines here are two different designed forms, not one working and one
absent. HUM LEAD saw exactly what was built.

**What the sitting establishes is narrower and still real.** In colour the field answered
both halves of the question in one reading: *"alabama coolest with yellow, south florida
warmest with orange"*. In no colour the contours carry the field's **shape** - where the
bands lie - but nothing on them carries a **value**, so:

- *which band the place is in* has no answer in the frame: the contours are unlabelled,
  and a contour map normally labels its lines;
- *which way it gets warmer* was answerable in this scenario only because the scenario
  file places a marker named "Warmer" (`cmd/tuimaps/scenarios/scenario-4.json`). **That is
  scenario data, not something the library's no-colour form supplies** - a host drawing a
  temperature field with no such marker would get contours and no direction at all.

**And it did not function even where it was present:** HUM LEAD's blind readings of both
no-colour frames report lines and no direction, and do not mention the "Warmer" marker.
Told afterwards that it was there, HUM LEAD confirmed: *"I can find the warmer label - otherwise
'cannot tell' the direction of the contours on their own."* **So the marker is findable once you
know to look for it, and the contours alone do not carry the direction.** "Cannot tell" is a
finding under the sitting's own rule, not a pass.

So the question for REVIEW is not "is there a no-colour form" - there is - but **whether
an unlabelled contour is enough**, and whether the direction of increase should come from
the library rather than from whoever wrote the scenario.

**A contrast worth recording, from scenario 6 against scenario 4.** Where an overlay is
*points with labels* - an earthquake and a fire - taking colour away cost nothing: HUM LEAD
read direction for both at both sizes and said of the no-colour frame only *"Same; just no
color, but labels are the same."* Where an overlay is a *field*, taking colour away cost
the question itself (Finding 4). **The no-colour weakness is not general; it is specific to
ranked, filled things** - fields, and to a lesser degree the radar's levels (Finding 2).
That narrows what REVIEW has to fix.

**Refining Finding 3.** Scenario 6 shows the picture carrying distance after all - as a
*comparison*. Blind, HUM LEAD read the earthquake as near and to the north-west and the
fire as "significantly farther away to the southeast". So the division of labour is
sharper than Finding 3 first stated it: **the picture carries order and relative
magnitude - which is nearer, which is bigger, which way - and the words carry the
quantity.** That is a defensible split for a terminal map rather than a shortcoming, and
it is the same split D-67 relies on. Findings 1 and 3 stand, but what they ask of REVIEW is
whether the *names* and the *numbers* are reachable when wanted, not whether the picture
should have carried them.

## Finding 5 - a distance judged against another frame was wrong by five times (14.15, scenarios 6 and 7)

**Blind, HUM LEAD judged the storm track's closest approach as** *"relatively close ... similar to the
earthquake distance in the previous scenarios"*. The keys do not agree:

| | ground distance | separation on the 149x38 frame |
|---|---|---|
| Scenario 6, the earthquake | 6.0 km | 1.87 cells |
| Scenario 7, the storm track | 31.9 km | 9.96 cells |

The storm track is **5.3 times farther**, in kilometres and in cells alike, and both frames
were drawn at the same size. So this is not the scale bar changing underfoot between
frames; on screen the two separations were about two cells and about ten.

**What it is evidence of is not yet established, and there are two candidates.** The first
is that a comparison held in memory across two scenarios is simply not the same act as a
comparison made inside one frame - scenario 6's own internal comparison, near earthquake
against far fire, was read correctly and confidently. The second is that the closest point
of a *track* is harder to locate than a point marker, so the eye takes the track's nearest
visible stretch rather than its true closest approach. **The sitting cannot separate these
two**, and the difference matters: the first is a limit on how people read maps and needs
no fix, the second is a real weakness in how a track is drawn.

Recorded for REVIEW with that question open. It also qualifies the note under scenario 6:
the picture carries relative magnitude **within a frame**, and this is the one reading in
the sitting where a cross-frame comparison was attempted and it failed.

**The frame approvals are qualified, and are recorded as HUM LEAD gave them.** Asked
whether the twenty-four frames become goldens, HUM LEAD answered *"approved for now"*.
They are therefore goldens, and the qualification is kept with them: an approval given
while five findings are open, two of which (Findings 4 and 5) could change what a frame
should look like. If either is acted on, the frames it touches are written again with
`scripts/uat --frames` and are candidates once more (RS-15).

**The one contradiction in the sitting, and how HUM LEAD settled it.** For scenario 4's two
no-colour screens HUM LEAD read, blind and again after being shown where the marker was,
*"'cannot tell' the direction of the contours on their own"*, and then gave *"Pass for all"*
when verdicts were taken. Put back to them, HUM LEAD ruled: **"Cannot tell on that scenario
stands."**

**So the sitting's outcome is 22 passes and 2 cannot-tells, not 24 passes**, and the two
are a field drawn without colour. Under the sitting's own rule that is a finding. **NFR-15
- the map works without colour - is therefore not met for temperature fields**, and
Finding 4 is a candidate to block the v0.1.0 tag rather than post-tag work. That decision
is HUM LEAD's and has not been taken.

## Finding 6 - "no data here" tells a listener the wrong thing (14.17, scenario 3)

The description for scenario 3 is `radar: no data here, heavier 293 kilometres to the east`,
and the key agrees with it exactly: the radar image has class 0 at that place, which is
*no data*. So it is **true**, and nothing was mispronounced.

The trouble is what a person hears. **"No data here" sounds like the system failed** - a
feed that did not arrive, a broken sensor - when what it means is "this spot is outside
what the radar covers". A listener deciding whether to worry about rain is told, in effect,
that something is broken. The global standard this project works to says an error must say
what happened, why, and what to do next, and never show a raw technical state to a
non-technical reader; "no data" is the raw state.

For REVIEW: whether the phrase should distinguish *outside the radar's reach* from *the
radar looked and found nothing*, which are different facts and matter differently.

## Finding 7 - an annotation is described as a place (14.17, scenario 4)

Scenario 4's description has two entries: `Home - temperature: 15 degrees Celsius, band 10,
rising to the south-east`, and `Warmer - temperature: 23 degrees Celsius, band 11, rising
to the south-east`. **"Warmer" is not a place.** It is the direction annotation the
scenario file places to say which way the field increases (Finding 4), and the library,
which has no way to know that, describes it as a place with a temperature of its own.

This is a scenario-authoring artefact rather than a library defect, and it is the same
thread as Finding 4: the direction of increase is being carried by a marker pretending to
be a place, and it shows in both halves - as an unlabelled contour in the picture and as a
phantom place in the words. **HUM LEAD also judged the scenario 4 description "long"**,
which matters more in speech than on a screen: a listener cannot skim.

## What the descriptions do to Finding 4, and to the tag

**The words answer what the no-colour picture could not.** Scenario 4's description gives
the value, the band and the direction outright - *"15 degrees Celsius, band 10, rising to
the south-east"* - which is precisely the question HUM LEAD could not answer from the
contours. So a person with no colour is not left without an answer; they are left without
an answer **in the picture**.

That distinction is the whole of the tag decision and must not be blurred:

- **NFR-15, as the plan words it, is about the frames alone** - "the M1 questions answered
  from the no-colour frames alone". By that wording it is **not met** for a field, and
  HUM LEAD's "cannot tell" stands.
- **The practical harm is smaller than that sounds**, because describe mode carries the
  band and the direction for anyone who asks for them.

Whether v0.1.0 ships with a field that needs its words is HUM LEAD's ruling and has not
been taken.

### The one place where the words say what the picture cannot (14.17, scenario 2)

Scenario 2's description ends with **"closer than 1 cell of the map"**. That is the library
telling the reader, in the reader's own terms, where the picture's resolution runs out.

Set it against what the sitting recorded hours earlier. Blind, at all four sizes and colour
depths, HUM LEAD read the place as *inside* and on the edge, and said of the direction that
*"that level of precision is difficult-to-improbable for a human viewer looking at a
terminal created map using our characters"*. The description answers exactly that: the
direction is **north**, the distance is **0.3 km**, and it volunteers that this is **under
one cell** - so the reader is told both the answer and why the picture could not have given
it.

**This is D-67 working as designed**, and it is the strongest evidence in the record for the
division of labour Findings 1, 3 and 5 circle around: the picture places, the words settle,
and the words admit when the picture could not. Recorded here because a sitting that only
collects findings gives a false account of what was built.

### The compass question scenario 6 left open, settled by the words

In the M1a sitting HUM LEAD read the earthquake as *"upper left (Northweat)"*. The key puts
it at **bearing 343 degrees, compass north**, and the spoken description agrees: *"6km to
the north"*. So the picture reading was **one sector off** on the eight-point compass -
343 degrees falls inside north, which runs from 337.5 to 22.5.

**It is not a failure of the frame.** The earthquake sits 1.87 cells from the place at
149x38, and a seventeen-degree error at that separation is far inside one cell - well
within "the frame's own resolution", which is the standard D-67 sets. Drawn at that
distance, "slightly left of straight up" is an honest description of 343 degrees. The
verdict of pass for scenario 6 therefore stands, and the reading is recorded as further
evidence for the same division of labour: **the picture gives the quadrant, the words give
the bearing.**

### Why the screen-reader half is not recorded here (D-122)

A description is **data**: `describe.Answer` carries the place, the overlay, the relation,
the distance and unit, the bearing and compass word, and the label. The tie between a place
and its facts is in the structure, not in the two spaces the app prints in front of them.
So a screen reader run against a terminal would be testing the terminal emulator's
accessibility tree, not this library's contract - and the coordinator's earlier worry that
a reader might "flatten the indentation" mistook a convenience printout for the interface.

**The debt is not cancelled, only moved.** PL-AX-3 is owed at task 14.19, where a host
renders `Answer` values to its own platform's accessibility layer. If that review does not
test it, this paragraph is where the gap shows.
